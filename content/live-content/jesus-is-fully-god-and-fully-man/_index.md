Our topic for discussion:

> When Mary supernaturally conceived by the power of the Holy Spirit, there was a physical human child in her womb. But Jesus has always been the divine second person of the Trinity. So how does His nature change as part of the incarnation? Did anything happen at conception, or just at birth? Does Jesus now have a human nature, a divine nature, or both?

## The fetus in Mary's womb did not yet have a full human nature, because the human spirit is given at birth, not conception

{{< properties

stags="Human beings > Are dichtomous, composed of flesh and spirit | Human beings > Are not trichotomous (we do not have souls, but are souls) | Soul > Is the combination of flesh and spirit | Human beings > The human spirit is given at birth, not conception | Mary > Is the mother of Jesus's humanity, not His divinity"

ptags="John 3:6"

>}}

As [Ichthys](https://ichthys.com/) teaches, the human spirit is given by God at birth, not conception. Some links:

- [SR3: The Purpose, Creation and Fall of Man, §The Human Spirit](https://ichthys.com/Fall-sr3.htm#3.%20The%20Human%20Spirit)
- [Email: Life begins at birth not conception](https://ichthys.com/mail-life-begins-at-birth.htm)
- [Email: Life begins at birth not conception](https://ichthys.com/mail-Culture-and-ChristianityIV.htm#life%20begins%20at%20birth%20not%20conception)

Human beings are dichotomous in nature, being composed of both flesh and spirit. Properly speaking, Mary was the mother (in the biological sense) of Jesus's flesh, and not his human spirit (since the human spirit is given directly by the hand of God at the point of birth).

It might seem sort of strange to emphasize this point, but we have done so to make the following argument:

> The fact that the fetus in Mary's womb did not yet even have a *human* spirit makes notions of Mary carrying the divine second person of the Trinity in her womb patently ridiculous.

Do people actually teach something along those lines? Yes, unfortunately. The Catholics and Orthodox call Mary "*theotokos*" ("God-bearer"), and say that she is the "Mother of God" (cf. the wording of the Catholics' Hail Mary prayer: "Holy Mary, Mother of God, pray for us sinners...").

All of this is very problematic theologically. As John 3:6 puts it, "that which is born from the flesh is flesh, and that which is born from the Spirit is spirit." How then can Mary (who was flesh) be said to be in any way mother to God (who is spirit; cf. John 4:24)? It just don't not compute, biblically.
